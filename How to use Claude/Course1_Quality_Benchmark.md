# Course 1 Quality Benchmark

*Every Course 1 module must meet the following standard before it is mapped into Jifunze. The four modules above are the reference. The remaining modules must reach the same bar.*

## 1. What every module must contain
Each module includes, in order: a *What was preserved* note, a *What was improved* note, an *Estimated time*, a *Module purpose*, *Learner outcomes* (three to six), a *Core lesson* in clear prose with named sub-sections, *Fully written worked examples* (at least four for keep/improve modules, at least four for rewrite modules, with full text — not just titles), *Supplied practice activity materials* (the actual content learners need, not activity names), a *Pause and check* (two or three self-check questions), a *Knowledge-to-output task* that produces the portfolio artifact, an eight-question *Checkpoint quiz* spanning multiple-choice, scenario, short-answer, and application formats, an *Answer key with explanations*, a named *Portfolio artifact* with a saved filename, a *Capstone-save reminder* tying the artifact forward, *Module completion evidence*, *Revision guidance*, and a *Transition to the next module*. No module skips any of these sections.

## 2. What a fully written worked example must include
A fully written worked example contains the actual prompt sent to AI, the actual AI output (or a realistic facsimile), a *what sounds correct* observation, a *what is wrong* analysis with at least two specific points, an *improved version* where applicable, and a *what the learner should notice* statement. Worked examples that consist only of a title (e.g., *Worked example 2: Customer communication scenario*) do not meet the benchmark. The reader should be able to learn from the example without going outside the document.

## 3. What supplied practice materials must include
Practice materials supply the source content. If the activity asks learners to sort statements, the statements are listed. If the activity asks learners to compare outputs, the outputs are written. If the activity asks learners to verify claims, the claims are provided. The minimum quantity is enough for the learner to do the activity meaningfully — typically eight to ten items for sorting, two to three full output sets for comparison, and one or two source documents for any document-based task. Activities that say *"the facilitator will provide…"* or *"choose any scenario…"* without supplying examples do not meet the benchmark.

## 4. What makes a checkpoint acceptable
A checkpoint contains at least eight scored items in a mix of formats: at least two multiple-choice, at least two scenario-based, at least two short-answer, and at least one application item that requires the learner to do something with module content rather than recall it. Each item must align to a specific learner outcome from the module. The answer key explains why the correct answer is correct, not only which option is correct, and gives the criteria for strong scenario and application answers. The checkpoint pass threshold is at least 6 of 8 questions correct.

## 5. What makes a portfolio artifact credible
A credible portfolio artifact is concrete, specific to the learner's real context, and reviewable by someone who was not in the lesson. It has a defined format, a saved filename pattern (e.g., `Module07_Audience_Fit_Rewrites_[YourName].pdf`), and at least one clear acceptance criterion the learner can self-check against. It connects to a future module — usually the capstone — so the learner sees why it matters. Generic or templated artifacts that any learner could produce identically are not credible portfolio evidence.

## 6. What self-learner guidance must include
Self-learner guidance assumes no facilitator is present. It replaces *"the facilitator will introduce…"* with *"start by reading…"*. Every "Misconceptions to listen for during facilitation" section is replaced with a self-check rubric. Every "Peer or facilitator critique" activity has a documented solo alternative. Every section that uses team or group framing offers a single-learner path. The pause-and-check questions, the revision guidance, and the answer key together form the substitute for the facilitator's listening.

## 7. What tone and style must be used
Natural, practical, serious. Direct sentences. No marketing words like "premium," "world-class," or "transformative." No motivational filler ("This module will change how you work!"). No promises about jobs, income, or accreditation. No "you'll absolutely love this." Examples are practical and recognizable for learners in Kenya — KES, Nairobi, Safaricom, KRA, iTax, Kenyatta University — and remain understandable for a global reader. Cultural references should not require explanation, but they should also not assume a Western default. The reading level is professional but accessible: technical terms are defined the first time they appear, and complex ideas are followed by a concrete example.

## 8. What boilerplate must be removed
The following must not appear in learner-facing modules:

- The repeated sixteen-times "Intervene only if the learner shows overtrust…" paragraph.
- The "Premium completion upgrade" header and content block.
- The repeated "Self-learner success path" / "Evidence of completion" / "Automated/self-paced checkpoint recommendation" / "Minimal facilitator intervention points" five-block tail.
- The "Validation and quality assurance checklist" and "Validation check" sections.
- The phrase "It is aligned to the same premium authoring standard."
- Any sentence whose only function is to compliment the course on itself.

The Misconceptions, Differentiation, and Facilitator-intervention guidance is moved to a separate `Course1_Facilitator_Guide.md`. The Validation Checklist content is moved to a separate `Course1_QA_Checklist.md`. The per-module checkpoint specifications are moved to `Course1_Assessment_Standards.md`.

## 9. What employability and pathway language should look like
Each module includes one explicit pathway connection — a sentence or short paragraph that names which Jifunze pathways the module's artifact most directly supports (Digital Work Starter, AI Productivity Professional, Remote Work and Freelancing, Small Business and Entrepreneurship, Junior Tech Builder). The language is concrete: *"This artifact is a usable proof point for the Digital Work Starter pathway, where learners need to demonstrate they can apply AI safely in office and operations roles."* Avoid claims about getting jobs, earning a specific income, or being certified to a level the course does not actually certify. The course produces evidence the learner can use; it does not guarantee outcomes.

## 10. What ready-for-Cursor-mapping means
A module is ready for platform mapping when:

- It can be split into the eight platform lesson blocks (overview, concept, guided example, practice, reflection, checkpoint, artifact upload, remediation) without rewriting the core teaching.
- Every checkpoint question has a structure suitable for UI conversion (clear stem, defined response format, machine-checkable or rubric-checkable answer).
- The portfolio artifact has a defined upload format (.pdf, .docx, .md) and a filename convention.
- The transition into the next module is a single short paragraph that fits as a closing UI block.
- The remediation guidance is specific enough to power an automated "review section X" link rather than a generic "study more" prompt.
- The module's text contains no facilitator-only instructions, no "ask the group," and no embedded references to in-person delivery.

When a module meets all of items 1 through 10, it has reached the benchmark. The first four modules above are the reference. Every subsequent rewrite, improvement, or polish target should be checked against this list before it is considered done.
