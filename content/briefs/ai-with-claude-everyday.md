## 2. Target learner

Picture three concrete archetypes:

- **Archetype 1:** A mid-career project manager at a hospital who has tried ChatGPT and Claude a few times to summarize meeting notes, found them inconsistent, and stopped using them for anything that matters. Wants to know whether AI is genuinely useful for her work or whether she's right to be skeptical.
- **Archetype 2:** A 28-year-old marketing analyst who uses Claude daily for first drafts and feels increasingly uncomfortable about how much of his deliverable is AI-touched without verification. Wants a defensible workflow he can describe to his manager.
- **Archetype 3:** A first-year associate at a consulting firm whose firm just rolled out a Claude Enterprise license with vague guidance and zero training. Wants to use it well, fast, before he develops bad habits.

**Most representative learner in one sentence:** A knowledge worker who has Claude available, has used it inconsistently, and wants to integrate it into their work with judgment rather than enthusiasm or fear.

## 3. Prerequisites

- **Required:** Comfortable using a computer for knowledge work; has access to Claude (free or paid tier).
- **Helpful but not required:** Has tried Claude or ChatGPT at least once. No coding background assumed. No prior AI/ML knowledge assumed.

## 4. What the learner will be able to do after completion

- Distinguish where Claude reliably helps from where it predictably fails on real work tasks.
- Build and apply a verification routine appropriate to the stakes of each task.
- Integrate Claude into one or more real work workflows with explicit quality gates.
- Defend their AI use stance to a skeptical colleague, manager, or regulator.
- Recognize and avoid the four most common failure modes: confident hallucination, drift toward AI-dependent judgment, over-investment in prompt complexity, and using AI on tasks where the verification cost exceeds the time saved.
- Update their stance and workflow as Claude's capabilities change over the next 12–24 months.

## 5. Portfolio outputs the learner produces

- A personal **use-stance one-pager** — what they will and will not use Claude for, with reasoning.
- A **verification matrix** mapping task risk to verification depth.
- A **workflow integration plan** for one specific recurring work task, with quality gates and a 30-day measurement plan.
- A **failure-mode incident log** with three documented near-misses from their own use, annotated with what they learned.

## 6. Capstone description

- **Capstone task:** Submit a polished, team-ready document titled "My Claude use stance" that integrates all four portfolio outputs into a single 3–5 page memo a manager could read in 10 minutes. The memo should be defensible: a skeptical reader should be able to follow the reasoning even if they disagree with the conclusions.
- **Evidence filename:** `Module04_Use_Stance_[YourName].pdf`
- **Acceptance criteria:**
  - A clear, specific use stance (not generic AI principles)
  - At least three named verification techniques the learner actually applies
  - One real workflow with quality gates and metrics
  - At least three documented near-miss incidents with lessons drawn
  - The memo is written for the learner's actual workplace, not a fictional company
  - Length: 800–1500 words

## 7. Course shape — modules

Four modules, ~3–5 lessons each. Total ~14–18 lessons.

- **Module 1: What Claude Is and What It Is Not** — Build the mental model. Probabilistic helper, not search, not automation, not an oracle. Why Claude can sound confident and be wrong. Task patterns it handles reliably vs predictably poorly.

- **Module 2: Verification as a Core Skill** — When and how to verify Claude's outputs. The stakes-to-verification mapping. Three named verification techniques (source check, recomputation, adversarial reading). Practice on the learner's own real work.

- **Module 3: Integrating Claude into Real Workflows** — Pick one recurring work task. Design a workflow with quality gates. Measure baseline vs Claude-augmented performance for 30 days. Common integration anti-patterns and how to avoid them.

- **Module 4: Defensible Use — The Stance Memo** — Synthesize the previous modules into a personal use stance the learner can show their manager. Failure-mode incident log. Plan for staying current as Claude evolves.

## 8. Voice notes specific to this course

- This is *not* a "Claude is amazing, you should use it more" course. The voice is calmly skeptical-then-positive: take the technology seriously, take its failure modes equally seriously.
- Reference *Claude specifically* (Sonnet 4.6, Opus 4.6, Haiku 4.5 model names where relevant) — not "modern LLMs" generically.
- Worked examples should use realistic professional contexts: drafting an executive update, reviewing a contract clause, summarizing a 40-page report, generating a project timeline. Not "write me a poem about a robot."
- Anthropic-specific features (Projects, Artifacts, the Constitutional AI training approach) can be referenced where relevant but the course should not assume Claude.ai vs Claude API vs Claude Desktop — the learner should be able to apply the lessons regardless of surface.

## 9. Authoritative references

- Anthropic's published model cards for Claude Sonnet 4.6 and Opus 4.6.
- Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy (most recent version).
- Anthropic's "How to use Claude" documentation.
- "Constitutional AI" paper (Bai et al., 2022) — for principled background on Claude's training approach.
- The METR evaluation results on Claude's autonomous capabilities (most recent).
- Daniel Schmachtenberger's writing on AI judgment trade-offs (general background, not Claude-specific).
- The learner's own real work documents (the central recurring input throughout the course).

## 10. What this course is NOT

- This course is not "an introduction to AI" or "an introduction to large language models." Take #1 AI Essentials for that foundation.
- This course is not Claude-API tutorials for developers. It's about Claude as a knowledge-work assistant, not a programming interface.
- This course does not cover prompt engineering as a deep technical skill — take #3 Prompt Engineering Across Models for that.
- This course does not teach how to build AI agents — take #4 Building AI Agents.
- This course does not cover AI safety from a societal or alignment-research perspective — take #5 AI Safety, Alignment, and Responsible Use.
- This course does not assume the learner has any specific role; it's deliberately broad so a PM, analyst, lawyer, marketer, or operator can all complete it.
