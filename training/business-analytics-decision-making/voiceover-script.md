## Module 1 — Business Analytics Foundations

### Lesson 1.1 — Course promise and business value

**Narration:**

This course exists to connect analytics work to decisions, not decoration. You are learning diagnosis: what changed, in which segments, with what evidence strength, and what management should do next.

### Lesson 1.2 — What business analytics means here

**Narration:**

Analytics here means disciplined inquiry. You translate messy reality into definitions, compare expectations to outcomes, and communicate uncertainty without freezing the business.

### Lesson 1.3 — Data, metrics, KPIs, insights, and action

**Narration:**

Keep vocabulary precise. Raw rows become metrics; metrics become KPIs when they steer decisions. An insight explains what changed and why it may matter. A recommendation names owners, timing, tradeoffs, and what you will monitor.

### Lesson 1.4 — The analytics thinking cycle

**Narration:**

Use a simple loop: frame the decision, measure, diagnose, decide, monitor. The loop prevents two common failures: chart gazing without a question, and premature action without measurement.

### Lesson 1.5 — Meet GlowCare Beauty & Retail

**Narration:**

GlowCare blends products and services across walk-in, online, social, and referrals. May is the focal month: revenue pressure, margin pressure, rising complaints, weaker repeat purchase, more stockouts, and more late deliveries. Hold those signals together as you listen forward.

### Lesson 1.6 — Business questions to investigate

**Narration:**

Pause and write three investigative questions for May, each spanning at least three metric families—financial, customer, and operational. Good questions force triangulation instead of single-cause stories.

## Module 2 — Understanding Business Performance Data

### Lesson 2.1 — Reading the core dataset

**Narration:**

Before you interpret, confirm the grain of time, the segment keys, and whether definitions changed month to month. Small definition shifts can fake trends.

### Lesson 2.2 — Raw data vs summarized data

**Narration:**

Summaries hide distributions. A stable average can mask two opposing segment stories. When May weakens, split by channel and mix before you explain “the business.”

### Lesson 2.3 — Metrics that matter for GlowCare

**Narration:**

Keep a compact map: revenue and gross margin, conversion and funnel drop-offs, repeat rate and complaints, stockouts and on-time delivery. Metrics should always point to a decision stake.

### Lesson 2.4 — KPI dashboard design

**Narration:**

Ask three questions for every KPI card: what denominator, what comparison period, and what operational counterpart belongs beside the headline number.

### Lesson 2.5 — June dashboard interpretation

**Narration:**

Green revenue against target can still hide execution pain. If complaints and stockouts worsen, your narrative must surface operational risk, not only celebrate the target.

## Module 3 — Trend and Variance Analysis

### Lesson 3.1 — Revenue, cost, and profit trend

**Narration:**

Read revenue with margin and absolute profit together. Revenue alone is a vanity lens when profitability is under pressure.

### Lesson 3.2 — Why profit matters more than revenue

**Narration:**

May illustrates the point: revenue stress plus cost pressure is a profitability diagnosis. That framing changes which levers you prioritize.

### Lesson 3.3 — Revenue and margin combo analysis

**Narration:**

When revenue and margin diverge, investigate mix, discounting, variable costs, and fulfillment friction. The combo chart is a conversation starter, not a verdict.

### Lesson 3.4 — Month-over-month variance analysis

**Narration:**

Quantify April to May deltas in plain language: direction, magnitude, and suspected driver families—without claiming a single root cause yet.

### Lesson 3.5 — May performance warning signs

**Narration:**

Margin down alongside complaints up, repeat down, and operations worsening is a aligned warning set. Alignment raises priority; it does not prove causality.

### Lesson 3.6 — April-to-May heatmap and profit waterfall

**Narration:**

Heatmaps localize weakness across segments. Waterfalls tell a driver story. Use both to decide where to dig next with operations and finance.

## Module 4 — Channel, Product, and Customer Analysis

### Lesson 4.1 — Revenue by channel

**Narration:**

Read channel bars with both share and trajectory. A large channel can be profitable yet fragile if execution weakens.

### Lesson 4.2 — Channel contribution and risk

**Narration:**

Online and delivery often concentrate reputation risk. When late deliveries rise, investigate promises, inventory, and handoffs for that channel specifically.

### Lesson 4.3 — Product/service revenue mix

**Narration:**

Stacked mix charts show strategic shifts. Services versus retail inventory stress different operations and margins—name that difference explicitly.

### Lesson 4.4 — Funnel analysis and conversion rate interpretation

**Narration:**

Stage-level failure is channel-specific. Do not average away a broken step; read traffic quality, errors, stock availability, and checkout friction where conversion dips.

### Lesson 4.5 — New vs returning customers

**Narration:**

Returning customers often anchor margin. Weakening repeat while complaints rise is a priority signal—treat it as a hypothesis to test with cohorts and timelines.

### Lesson 4.6 — Complaints vs repeat customer rate

**Narration:**

State one plausible mechanism linking complaints to repeat rate, and one alternative explanation. Professional analytics holds multiple hypotheses until evidence tightens.

## Module 5 — Diagnosing the Business Problem

### Lesson 5.1 — Operational issue trends

**Narration:**

Plot stockouts, late deliveries, and complaint resolution time alongside revenue and margin. Operational series are diagnostic companions, not footnotes.

### Lesson 5.2 — Connecting operations to customer behavior

**Narration:**

Mechanism thinking links execution failures to experience failures before they fully appear in revenue. That sequencing matters for credible recommendations.

### Lesson 5.3 — Root cause diagram for May

**Narration:**

Sketch a small cause tree with branches for operations, experience, and financial outcomes. Multi-factor framing protects the business from expensive single-fix mistakes.

### Lesson 5.4 — What the data tells us vs what it does not prove

**Narration:**

Prefer language like “consistent with,” “suggests,” and “requires validation” over “proves.” Executives trust analysts who know the limits of the dataset.

### Lesson 5.5 — Common beginner mistakes

**Narration:**

Avoid single-metric obsession, ignoring mix, misleading scales, and equating correlation with proof. Each mistake has a human cost in money and trust.

### Lesson 5.6 — Bad chart vs better chart

**Narration:**

Use the deck’s before-and-after visuals as a checklist: clear scales, honest comparisons, and charts matched to the decision at hand.

## Module 6 — Turning Analytics Into Action

### Lesson 6.1 — From observation to insight

**Narration:**

An insight states what you observe in which segment and period, and why it implies risk or opportunity because of a plausible mechanism.

### Lesson 6.2 — From insight to recommendation

**Narration:**

Recommendations carry owners, time horizon, cost and risk, and weekly monitoring metrics. Without owners, analysis evaporates after the meeting.

### Lesson 6.3 — Decision matrix

**Narration:**

Score a few candidate actions on speed, cost, and confidence given current evidence. Matrices make tradeoffs explicit; they do not remove accountability for assumptions.

### Lesson 6.4 — Executive recommendation for GlowCare

**Narration:**

Structure the storyline: situation, complication, key findings, recommendation, risks, and next thirty days. Practice delivering it in under two minutes.

### Lesson 6.5 — Learner practice: analyze the May drop

**Narration:**

Draft your GlowCare recommendation: facts, likely contributors, unknowns, and prioritized actions using revenue trend, cost trend, margin, complaints, repeat rate, stockouts, late deliveries, and average order value.

### Lesson 6.6 — Mini quiz + final recap

**Narration:**

Take the twelve-question quiz when you are ready. It tests interpretation, not memorization. Afterward, note three habits you will keep weekly—dashboard discipline, segment cuts, or safer claim language.
