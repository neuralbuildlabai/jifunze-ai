What is Prompt Engineering?

The art and science of crafting inputs that guide AI language models to produce desired outputs.

Why It Matters:

  • Save hours of back-and-forth
  • Produce more accurate and relevant results
  • Unlock hidden AI capabilities
  • Make AI applications reliable and useful
Key Insight: The same AI can give brilliant insights or complete nonsense—it all depends on how you ask.

The Power of Good Prompts

❌ Bad Prompt

"Write about dogs"

Result: Generic, unfocused content

✅ Good Prompt

"Write a 200-word article explaining why dogs make excellent therapy animals for children with autism. Include specific behavioral traits and cite recent research."

Result: Focused, detailed, actionable content

The Difference: Clarity, Specificity, and Structure

5 Core Principles

1. 🎯 Clarity - Be Specific

Never assume the AI knows your context

2. 📚 Context - Provide Background

Set the stage with relevant details

3. 🏗️ Structure - Format Matters

Organize your prompt like the output you want

4. 🎚️ Constraints - Set Boundaries

Boundaries improve creativity and relevance

5. 💡 Examples - Show Don't Tell

One good example beats a thousand words

Principle 1: Clarity

Vague:

"Help me with my email"

Clear:

"Write a professional email to my client apologizing for a delayed project delivery. The delay was due to unexpected technical issues. Maintain a positive tone and propose a new deadline of next Friday."

Specify: Type of content • Recipient • Purpose • Reason • Tone • Action

Principle 2: Context

Without Context:

"Explain quantum computing"

With Context:

"I'm a high school teacher preparing a lesson for 10th graders who have basic knowledge of atoms and electricity. Explain quantum computing using simple analogies they can relate to. Keep it under 300 words."

Context tells the AI: Your role • Your audience • Their knowledge level • Desired approach • Constraints

Principle 3: Structure

Unstructured:

"I need a business plan for a coffee shop with financial projections and marketing strategy and competitive analysis"

Structured:

Create a business plan for a specialty coffee shop with the following sections: 1. Executive Summary (150 words) 2. Market Analysis - Target demographic - Competitive landscape 3. Marketing Strategy - Social media approach - Local partnerships 4. Financial Projections - Startup costs - 3-year revenue forecast Use bullet points for clarity.

Principles 4 & 5: Constraints & Examples

Constraints Focus the AI

  • Length: Word count, character limit
  • Tone: Professional, casual, humorous
  • Format: Bullets, paragraphs, tables
  • Audience: Age, expertise level
  • Style: Technical, conversational

Examples Guide Output

Write product descriptions in our brand voice. Example: Product: Wireless Earbuds Our Style: "Meet your new workout buddy. These earbuds laugh in the face of sweat..." Now write for: Smartwatch

Zero-Shot vs Few-Shot

Zero-Shot

"Translate this English text to French: The weather is beautiful today."

When to use:

  • Simple tasks
  • Common operations
  • Quick requests

Few-Shot

Convert reviews to sentiment scores: Review: "Exceeded expectations!" Sentiment: 5 Review: "It's okay." Sentiment: 3 Review: "Great value!" Sentiment: ?

When to use:

  • Custom formats
  • Consistent output
  • Domain-specific tasks

Chain-of-Thought Prompting

Ask the AI to show its reasoning process for complex problems

Without CoT:

"If a store has 15 apples and sells 40%, then receives 8 more, how many apples?"

May give wrong answer

With CoT:

"If a store has 15 apples and sells 40%, then receives 8 more, how many apples? Let's solve this step by step."

AI shows work:
1. Starting: 15
2. Sold: 40% × 15 = 6
3. Remaining: 15 - 6 = 9
4. New: 8
5. Total: 9 + 8 = 17

Key Phrases: "Let's think step by step" • "Let's solve this systematically" • "Show your work"

Role & Iterative Prompting

Role Prompting

"You are a blockchain expert with 10 years of experience explaining complex concepts to business executives. Explain blockchain technology highlighting business value, avoiding technical jargon."

Effective Roles:

  • Expert roles
  • Teacher roles
  • Professional roles
  • Creative roles

Iterative Refinement

First: "Write a tagline for an eco-friendly water bottle" Refine: "Make it more playful, targeting millennials" Further: "Good! Now create 3 variations with different tones"

Benefits: Faster than rewriting, builds on what works

Template & Negative Prompting

Template Prompting

Task: [Specific task] Context: [Background] Format: [Output structure] Constraints: [Requirements] Example: [Sample output]

Benefits:

  • Consistency
  • Reusability
  • Scalability

Negative Prompting

"Write a professional email to a client. Do NOT: - Use overly formal language - Include unnecessary apologies - Make promises we can't keep - Use corporate jargon - Exceed 150 words"

When to use: Avoiding common mistakes, preventing unwanted styles

Comparative Prompting

Ask the AI to analyze differences or generate multiple options side by side.

Compare Options

"Compare Python and JavaScript for building a web scraper. Create a table covering: - Ease of learning - Library support - Performance - Best use cases"

Multiple Versions

"Write three versions of this headline: 1. Professional and authoritative 2. Casual and friendly 3. Urgent and action-oriented Topic: New cybersecurity software launch"

Use it for: A/B testing • Decision-making • Surfacing trade-offs • Creative variations

Advanced Patterns

System vs User Prompts

System Prompt:

You are a helpful coding assistant specializing in Python. You provide clear, well-commented code examples. You explain concepts using simple analogies.

Purpose: Sets persistent behavior

User Prompt:

Write a function to validate email addresses

Purpose: Specific task request

Analogy: System prompts are hiring instructions; user prompts are daily tasks

Parameter Tuning

Temperature Control (0.0 - 2.0)

Low (0.0-0.3): Focused, deterministic, consistent

Use for: Code, data analysis, factual content

Medium (0.4-0.7): Balanced creativity and consistency

Use for: General writing, explanations

High (0.8-1.2): Creative, varied, unpredictable

Use for: Brainstorming, creative writing, ideation

Other Parameters: max_tokens • top_p • frequency_penalty • presence_penalty

Prompt Chaining

Break complex tasks into sequential steps

Example: Research Report Generation

Step 1 - Outline: Create report outline with 5 sections

↓

Step 2 - Research: List key facts for each section

↓

Step 3 - Writing: Write 300-word sections using facts

↓

Step 4 - Polish: Improve clarity and add transitions

Benefits: Handles complexity • Maintains quality • Easier to debug • More control

Meta Prompting

Ask the AI to help you write a better prompt.

You ask:

"I want to generate creative product names for a line of organic skincare. Help me write a prompt that consistently produces: - 2-3 word names - Nature-inspired themes - Easy to pronounce - Available as .com domains What prompt should I use?"

AI returns:

"Generate 10 product names for organic skincare. Each should: - Be 2-3 words max - Reference plants, minerals, or seasons - Sound premium but approachable - Avoid skincare clichés Return each with a brief explanation."

When to use: Learning prompt engineering • Optimizing existing prompts • Getting unstuck

Common Pitfalls

1. Being Too Vague

❌ "Write something about marketing"
✅ "Write a 500-word blog post about email marketing best practices for small businesses"

2. Assuming Context

❌ "Fix this code" (without showing code)
✅ "Here's my Python code: [code]. It's giving a TypeError. Please fix and explain."

3. Ignoring Format

❌ "List the benefits of exercise"
✅ "List 5 benefits of exercise. Format: Benefit name (bold) + 2-sentence explanation"

4. Not Iterating

❌ Accepting first response
✅ Asking follow-up questions to improve results

Best Practices Checklist

Before Writing:

  • Define your goal clearly
  • Identify your audience
  • Determine desired format
  • Consider constraints

While Writing:

  • Be specific and explicit
  • Provide relevant context
  • Use clear structure
  • Include examples when helpful
  • Set appropriate constraints

After Writing:

  • Test with variations
  • Iterate based on results
  • Document what works
  • Refine for consistency

For Production:

  • Use templates for recurring tasks
  • Version control your prompts
  • Monitor output quality
  • Gather user feedback
  • Continuously improve
