# Scrum Made Simple: Plan Your First Sprint

A Jifunze.ai micro-course / workshop package.

## Course purpose

Most beginner Scrum material is written for software teams already inside an Agile environment, and most certification material is dense with jargon. This micro-course strips Scrum back to its working parts and walks the learner all the way through planning a real first sprint by the time the workshop ends.

The promise is small and concrete: in 35–45 minutes the learner can explain Scrum in plain language and produce a complete first sprint plan with a product goal, backlog, sprint goal, sprint backlog, task board, Definition of Done, review plan, and retrospective questions.

## Target learner

This package is built for people who are *not* on a software engineering team and *not* preparing for a certification exam. Specifically:

- Complete beginners who have heard the words "Agile" or "Scrum" and want a clean explanation
- Small business owners running a small team or a solo project
- Students working on group assignments or capstone projects
- Project assistants and operations coordinators
- New team leads taking on their first delivery responsibility
- Community organizers planning events, drives, or programs
- Non-technical learners who need to collaborate with a team that already uses Scrum

No prior project management knowledge is assumed.

## Duration

35–45 minutes of guided narration plus optional workbook time. Most learners finish the full package, including the mini-project, in 60–75 minutes.

## Course type

Micro-course / workshop. Self-paced with optional facilitator guidance for live delivery.

## Prerequisite

None. A pen and the workbook (printed or digital) are the only required tools.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the workshop, the learner will be able to:

1. Explain in plain language what Agile is and how Scrum sits inside it.
2. Describe the three Scrum accountabilities (Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developers) without certification jargon.
3. Describe the five Scrum events and what each one is for.
4. Write a clear product goal and a single-sentence sprint goal.
5. Build a product backlog of user stories with acceptance criteria.
6. Select a sprint backlog that fits the team's capacity.
7. Set up a simple task board with To Do / In Progress / Done columns.
8. Define a Definition of Done that fits the work being delivered.
9. Plan a sprint review and run a basic sprint retrospective.
10. Produce a complete first-sprint plan for a project of their own choosing.

## What this is not

This is **not** a Scrum certification course. It does not prepare a learner for the Professional Scrum Master (PSM), Certified ScrumMaster (CSM), Professional Scrum Product Owner (PSPO), or any other certification exam. We use plain-language explanations and clearly note that the official Scrum Guide uses the terms *accountabilities*, *events*, *artifacts*, and *commitments* — but we explain those in everyday words first.

Learners who want certification should be referred to scrumguides.org and an accredited training provider.
