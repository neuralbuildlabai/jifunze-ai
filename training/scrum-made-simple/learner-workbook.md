# Learner Workbook — Scrum Made Simple: Plan Your First Sprint

**How to use this workbook**

This workbook is your working document. By the end of the workshop, every section should be filled in for one real project of your own choosing. You can print it, write in a notebook, or fill it in digitally — the format does not matter, the doing does.

The workshop voiceover will tell you when to stop and write. Every prompt that needs your input is marked with a `▢` checkbox. When you have completed a prompt, tick the box.

There are three parts:

1. **Course notes** — space to capture key ideas and the four worked examples.
2. **Reflection and warm-up worksheets** — short prompts before the mini project.
3. **Mini project: your first sprint plan** — every artifact, end to end.

The last page is the *Mini-project submission checklist*. Use it to confirm nothing is missing.

# Part 2 — Reflection and warm-up worksheets

## User story practice

Before you build the real backlog, practise the shape with three throwaway examples. Pick any product you use every day (a streaming app, a coffee shop, a bus service) and write three user stories about it.

▢  Story A — As a ________________, I want ________________ so that __________________

▢  Story B — As a ________________, I want ________________ so that __________________

▢  Story C — As a ________________, I want ________________ so that __________________

## Acceptance criteria practice

Pick the strongest of your three throwaway stories. Write three short, testable acceptance criteria for it.

▢  AC1 — _____________________________________________________________

▢  AC2 — _____________________________________________________________

▢  AC3 — _____________________________________________________________

The test for a good acceptance criterion: a person who didn't write it can tell, without asking you, whether it has been met.

## 3.4 Sprint Goal worksheet

A sprint goal is one sentence. At the end of the sprint, you should be able to point at something and say "yes, we did that," or "no, we did not."

▢  My sprint goal: __________________________________________________
    ____________________________________________________________________

▢  Is it specific enough that we'll know if we hit it? ___________

▢  Is it small enough to finish in one sprint? __________________

If any answer is "no," sharpen the sentence and rewrite it above.

## 3.7 Definition of Done — checklist

The Definition of Done is the same for every item in this sprint. It is the team's quality bar.

For each box you tick, the work needs to meet that condition before it can move into the *Done* column.

Common items to consider:

▢  Reviewed by a second person on the team

▢  Acceptance criteria met for the relevant user story

▢  Tested in the way the work will actually be used

▢  Documented well enough that someone else could pick it up

▢  Visible to the customer / user / audience it is intended for

▢  Owner has confirmed it is complete

# Mini-project submission checklist

Before you call your sprint plan complete, tick every box.

▢  Product goal written
▢  Persona written
▢  Product backlog has 5–10 items as user stories, ordered by priority
▢  At least one item has acceptance criteria written out
▢  Sprint goal is a single sentence and is specific
▢  Sprint backlog has been selected and the team agrees it fits the sprint length
▢  Task board is set up (paper or digital)
▢  Definition of Done is written and at least 5 items long
▢  Daily Scrum has a time, place, and three questions
▢  Sprint Review has a date, an audience, and a question to ask
▢  Sprint Retrospective has a date and three questions
▢  All event dates are blocked in my real calendar
▢  At least one other person knows what my sprint goal is and when my review will be
▢  I have a clear sprint start date written here: __________________

If every box is ticked, you have a complete first-sprint plan. Print it, save it, share it. You are ready to start the sprint.
