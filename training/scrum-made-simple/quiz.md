# Final Quiz — Scrum Made Simple: Plan Your First Sprint

**Number of questions:** 15
**Format:** Multiple choice (MC), Scenario (S), Short answer (SA), Sequencing (SEQ)
**Recommended pass mark:** 80% (12 of 15 correct)
**Time:** 5–8 minutes

This is **not** a Scrum certification quiz. It is a self-check that the workshop's ideas have landed. If you score below 80%, the answer key tells you which lesson to revisit.

For scenario questions, choose the answer that most reflects what the *workshop* taught — there are sometimes good arguments for other answers in real life.

## Question 10 (S)

Maria runs a small coffee shop. She wants a website with: an online menu, a contact form, an Instagram feed, a blog, customer reviews, and a loyalty programme. She is planning her first sprint. Her team is two people working part-time. According to the workshop, what is the **best** next move?

## Question 11 (S)

B) Daily Scrum — they should add a fourth question.
C) Sprint Review — the stakeholders should give them harder feedback.

# Answer Key

| # | Format | Correct answer | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | MC | **B** | Agile is a philosophy of working that assumes the plan must adapt as the team learns. (Lesson 1) |
| 2 | MC | **C** | Agile is the broader philosophy; Scrum is one specific framework that implements it. (Lesson 1) |
| 3 | MC | **B** | The Product Owner owns the product backlog and decides priority. (Lesson 3) |
| 4 | MC | **C** | The Scrum Master does **not** assign work. The team self-organises. (Lesson 3) |
| 5 | SEQ | **3, 2, 1, 5, 4** | Order: Sprint (1, the container) → Sprint Planning (2) → Daily Scrum (3, repeated) → Sprint Review (4) → Sprint Retrospective (5). (Lesson 4) |
| 6 | S | **B** | Removing blockers is a core responsibility of the Scrum Master, and a blocker that has lasted two days needs immediate attention. (Lesson 3 + Lesson 4) |
| 7 | MC | **B** | The "As a / I want / So that" shape is the user story format taught in the workshop. (Lesson 5) |
| 8 | S | **B** | A good sprint goal is a single, testable outcome you can point at. A is too broad (uses "and"), C is vague, D is process-not-outcome. (Lesson 5) |
| 9 | SA | The team's shared, consistent definition of when an item is **really finished** — the quality bar applied to every item in the sprint. (Lesson 5) |
| 10 | S | **B** | Pick one outcome, narrow the sprint backlog to just the work that supports it. Don't overcommit. (Lesson 6) |
| 11 | S | **D** | Repeated misses are exactly what the Retrospective exists to surface and fix. (Lesson 4) |
| 12 | MC | **B** | To Do / In Progress / Done with optional Blocked column. (Lesson 5) |
| 13 | S | **C** | Mid-sprint changes go through the Product Owner. The sprint backlog is the team's commitment for the sprint. (Lesson 4 + Lesson 5) |
| 14 | SA | Any three of: product goal, persona, product backlog, sprint goal, sprint backlog, task board, Definition of Done, sprint review plan, sprint retrospective questions, Daily Scrum time/place. (Lesson 6) |
| 15 | S | **B** | Retrospectives end with one or two specific commitments — not a long list — so the team can actually act on them. (Lesson 4 + facilitator notes) |

# Scoring guide

- **15 / 15** — Excellent. You are ready to facilitate this for someone else.
- **12–14 / 15** — Pass. You have understood the workshop. Skim the answer key for the questions you missed.
- **9–11 / 15** — Borderline. Re-watch the lessons noted in the *Why* column, then retake.
- **0–8 / 15** — Don't worry. Most of the gap is usually one or two ideas. Re-do the workshop, paying special attention to the workbook prompts you skipped.

This score has no bearing on Scrum certification. It is a self-check only.
