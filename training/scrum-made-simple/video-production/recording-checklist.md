# Recording Checklist — Scrum Made Simple

A pre-recording, during-recording, and post-recording checklist for the voiceover artist and the producer. Read this once end-to-end before your first take.

---

## A. Pre-recording (do these the day before)

### Environment

▢  Pick a quiet room. Carpet, soft furniture, hung clothes — anything that absorbs sound. Avoid hard rooms (kitchens, bathrooms).
▢  Turn off fans, air conditioning, fridges, washing machines, dehumidifiers, anything that hums.
▢  Silence phones, notifications, smart speakers.
▢  Tell anyone in the house that recording is starting — confirm the time window.
▢  Close windows. Check for outdoor noise (lawn mowers, construction).
▢  If your setup is travel-grade, build a vocal booth: drape a heavy duvet over a clothes-rack frame around your microphone.

### Hardware

▢  Microphone tested with a 30-second test recording.
▢  Pop filter or windscreen in place.
▢  Microphone stand stable; no contact with the desk where typing happens.
▢  Headphones plugged in; check for monitoring delay.
▢  Power supply / batteries confirmed full or plugged in.

### Software

▢  Recording app set to 48 kHz, 24-bit WAV (or higher).
▢  Input level peaking around -12 to -6 dBFS during normal speech. Not louder.
▢  Background processes (browsers, Slack, sync apps) closed.
▢  Disk space confirmed (≥ 5 GB free for safety).
▢  Project file named: `scrum-made-simple_voiceover_v01.{rpp|logic|aup}`

### Script

▢  Print the script (`video-production/voiceover-script.md`) in 16pt or larger.
▢  Mark emphasis (underline) and breath points (slash marks) on the printed script.
▢  Highlight pause cues `[PAUSE — workbook]` so you know to leave silence, not narrate.
▢  Read the script out loud once at full pace before recording. Time it. Should land at 38–42 minutes.

### Voice prep

▢  Drink a glass of room-temperature water 30 minutes before. Avoid milk, fizzy drinks, caffeine spikes right before the session.
▢  Warm up: hum scales for 2 minutes, then read a paragraph aloud at full volume.
▢  Stand up if possible. Voice carries better standing.

---

## B. During recording

### General rules

▢  One take per paragraph minimum. Three takes per paragraph that introduces a new concept.
▢  When you fluff a line, **don't stop the recording** — pause for a beat, then re-do the sentence cleanly. The editor will pick the best take.
▢  Re-state the slide number aloud at the start of each section: "Slide 5." This makes editing dramatically easier.
▢  Leave 2 seconds of silence between paragraphs, and 5 seconds between lessons. Silence is a tool.

### Per-lesson workflow (recommended)

The script is broken into six lesson segments. Record one lesson per session, with a 5–10 minute break between. Six sessions can be done in one day if your voice is fresh.

| Session | Slides covered | Approx. recorded length |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Slides 1–10 (Cold open + Lesson 1) | ~6 min |
| 2 | Slides 11–15 (Lesson 2) | ~5 min |
| 3 | Slides 16–20 (Lesson 3) | ~6 min |
| 4 | Slides 21–25 (Lesson 4) | ~7 min |
| 5 | Slides 26–35 (Lesson 5) | ~8 min |
| 6 | Slides 36–43 (Lesson 6 + wrap) | ~10 min |

▢  Drink water between sessions. Don't push through hoarseness.

### Things to avoid

▢  No filler words: *um*, *uh*, *so*, *basically*, *right?*, *yeah*. Cut them in editing if they slip in.
▢  No apologetic openers: *I think*, *I mean*, *kind of*, *maybe*.
▢  No exclamation: *amazing*, *cool*, *love this*. Read the script as written.
▢  No paper noise. If you turn pages, do it in the silence between paragraphs.
▢  No clicks from your tongue or your jaw. Open your mouth more if you hear them.

### Pause cues

▢  When you reach `[PAUSE — workbook]`, stop speaking. Do not narrate over it. The editor will drop in 2 seconds of silence and an on-screen "Workbook" badge. Mark the time stamp aloud: "Pause cue, slide 10."

---

## C. Post-recording (same day)

### Immediate

▢  Bounce a backup of the raw recording before editing anything. Filename: `scrum-made-simple_RAW_YYYY-MM-DD.wav`.
▢  Move backup to a second drive or cloud folder.
▢  Listen back to the first 60 seconds at full volume on monitor speakers. Confirm no clicks, hum, or dropouts.

### Within 24 hours

▢  Edit out filler words, false starts, mouth clicks. Use the cleanest take of every paragraph.
▢  Normalise to -16 LUFS integrated loudness, peaks under -1 dBFS.
▢  Apply a gentle de-esser if "s" sounds are harsh.
▢  Apply gentle EQ — high-pass filter at 80 Hz to remove rumble; small dip around 250 Hz if voice sounds boxy; small lift at 5 kHz if voice sounds dull. Do not over-process.
▢  Save as 48 kHz / 24-bit WAV master, plus an MP3 reference for the editor.

### Hand-off

▢  Send the editor: master WAV, MP3 reference, the marked-up script, and a list of any places where you suggest a re-record.
▢  Note which slide each `[PAUSE — workbook]` cue should land on (use `slide-to-voiceover-map.md`).

---

## D. Re-record triggers

Re-record any take where:

▢  A whole sentence is mispronounced (especially "Scrum Master" said as "Scum Master" — happens more than you'd think).
▢  Voice sounds tired in the back half of a paragraph.
▢  Background noise leaked in.
▢  The pace dropped below ~120 wpm or rose above ~160 wpm.
▢  An example was named incorrectly (e.g. confusing "Amani" and "Daniel").
▢  Tone shifted unintentionally (e.g. drifted to lecture mode).

---

## E. Final readiness check (right before the editor takes over)

▢  All six lesson sessions recorded.
▢  Backup of raw recording exists in two locations.
▢  Edited master WAV is normalised and de-noised.
▢  Slide numbers are spoken at the start of each section in the WAV (for editing).
▢  Pause cues are silent (no narration over them).
▢  Total runtime is between 38 and 42 minutes.
▢  Script in `video-production/voiceover-script.md` matches what was actually recorded — note any improvised lines so the editor can update the captions.

When all of the above are ticked, the recording is ready for editing.
