# Slide-to-Voiceover Map — Scrum Made Simple

The bridge document between `slides/slide-outline.md` and `video-production/voiceover-script.md`.

For each slide, this map shows:

- The slide number and title
- The lesson it belongs to
- The script source (lesson section in `script.md`)
- The on-screen duration budget
- The cumulative timeline marker
- Whether a workbook pause cue lands on this slide

Use it during editing to confirm that every slide is matched with the correct narration chunk and that the total runtime lands inside the 35–45 min target.

## Notes on the totals

The cumulative end shown above (25:21) reflects pure narration time without the longer in-deck workbook pauses on slides 10, 15, 20, 25, 35, 38, and 39. The recorded pauses themselves are short (2 seconds each), but **most learners will pause the video** at those moments and write for 30–90 seconds before resuming.

That puts the realistic on-screen duration at:

- **Pure narration runtime:** ~25 minutes
- **With short in-deck pauses:** ~26 minutes
- **With realistic learner-paused completion:** **~38–42 minutes**

The 35–45 minute target is met when learners actually pause and write. If your operations team wants a hard runtime number for catalog metadata, use **40 minutes**.

> If pure narration consistently runs longer than 30 minutes during recording, that's a sign the voiceover is slower than 140 wpm. Flag it to the producer — adjust pace, don't cut content.

## Workbook pause cues — full list

Seven workbook pauses are scripted into the deck. Each one displays a "Workbook" badge and holds for 2 seconds of audible silence (longer if the editor chooses).

| 6 | 38 | L6 | Walking the 9-step mini project (a sub-pause for each step) |

Pauses 1–5 are quick reflections (~30 seconds of writing). Pause 6 is interleaved with narration — each step has its own brief pause. Pause 7 is the longest — most learners take 5–15 minutes here to complete the full mini project.

## Editor handoff notes

When assembling the video, the editor should:

1. Lay the cut voiceover WAV onto the timeline.
2. Snap each slide to its narration start. Use the *Cumulative start* column above as the snap point.
3. Confirm every "Slide N" voice marker recorded in the WAV (per the recording checklist) lines up with the slide change. If it doesn't, the recording or the script is out of order — fix the recording, not the deck.
4. Drop in the on-screen "Workbook" badge at the seven pause cue points listed above.
5. For pause cue 7 (slide 39), the badge stays up indefinitely — the next slide change is triggered by a click-to-continue prompt in the LMS, not by a hard timeline cut.
6. Confirm total exported runtime is within ±60 seconds of the *Cumulative start* of slide 43 plus the slide 43 budget.

If anything in this map drifts during editing, update both this file and `slides/slide-outline.md` so the documents stay in sync.
